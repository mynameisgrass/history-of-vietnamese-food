TRADITIONAL NOODLE SOUP

Phở

History

Originating in the late 19th century in Northern Vietnam (Nam Dinh and Hanoi), Phở is a beautiful fusion of Vietnamese rice noodles and French-influenced beef consumption. It became a staple for workers before evolving into the national dish.

When & Where

Traditionally enjoyed as a breakfast meal, Phở is now served 24/7 across the globe. The most authentic experience remains the street-side stalls of Hanoi's Old Quarter.

The soul of Phở lies in its broth—simmered for hours with charred ginger, onions, and a precise blend of star anise, cinnamon, and cardamom. It represents the resilience and adaptability of Vietnamese culture.

THE CRAFT

Preparation

Ingredients

• 2kg Beef bones (marrow/knuckle)
• 500g Beef brisket
• Charred ginger & onions
• Star anise, cinnamon, cardamom, cloves
• Flat rice noodles (Bánh phở)

Instructions

1. Parboil bones for 10 mins, then rinse.
2. Simmer bones with charred aromatics for 8-12 hours.
3. Toast spices and add to broth in the final 2 hours.
4. Season with fish sauce and rock sugar.
5. Assemble with blanched noodles and raw beef slices.

AUTUMN DELICACY

Cốm Làng Vòng

History

Tracing back over a thousand years to Vong Village (Hanoi), legend says during a heavy rainy season, locals harvested young rice to avoid starvation. They roasted and pounded it, creating the fragrant green rice flakes known as Cốm.

How it's used

Cốm is a delicate seasonal gift. It is often wrapped in lotus leaves to preserve its moisture and scent, usually eaten with ripe bananas or processed into cakes (Bánh Cốm) for traditional wedding ceremonies.

It is the very essence of Hanoi's autumn. The production process is a guarded secret, passed down through generations of artisans in the Vong lineage.

THE RITUAL

Traditional Serving

Selection

Choose the greenest flakes, ideally harvested in the early morning dew. The rice must be soft, tacky, and carry a distinct scent of young lotus and field grass.

The Pairing

Place a handful of Cốm on a lotus leaf. Pair it with a 'Chuối Tiêu' (pepper banana) that is perfectly ripe with small black spots. Dip the banana into the rice and savor slowly.
